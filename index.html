Overview

Riot was unifying its player ecosystem across three major titles into a single social layer — including cross-platform voice chat that extended to console. The challenge was designing a consistent, accessible component system that worked across wildly different input paradigms and screen contexts.

The console dimension made this especially complex: controller navigation, 10-foot display distance, and Sony/Microsoft certification requirements shaped every component decision from the ground up.

Visual System

↑ Example of muting someone in your party

Process
01
Component Architecture & Specification
Built out the component library for the voice chat system — defining anatomy, interaction states, usage guidelines, and platform-specific behavior across every context.
02
Designer Collaboration & Feedback Loops
Regular feedback huddles with the UX team — focused on understanding the why behind decisions, not just sign-off. That reasoning made me a stronger communicator throughout.
03
Engineering Feasibility
Every design went through a feasibility check with engineers. I learned to reframe proposals around tradeoffs — not "this is what I want," but "here's what this costs vs. what we gain."
04
Company-Wide Showcase
Presented the full system to 300+ Rioters at a company-wide capstone — months of iterative work distilled into a clear narrative.

Reflection

This capstone project was an invaluable experience. It taught me how to design for players, while making the system efficient and fluid. For example, when a player mutes themselves on a console controller, the other players know and can continue on the voice chat without them, or vice versa, when a player is speaking, the others can see their activity.

✓ What worked

The weekly stakeholder meetings forced me to synthesize messy in-progress work into clear decisions. In particular, the engineer feedback loop was invaluable: designing within real constraints is where the interesting problems live.

→ Next time

I'd invest more time understanding the design rationale early, asking my peer UX designers why a solution was chosen, what alternatives were considered, and what player research informed the decision, to better design for the players.

Overview

A full rebrand and design system for Neuron Digital — not just a visual refresh, but a scalable brand architecture built to grow with the company across products, platforms, and contexts.

The goal was coherence without rigidity: a system flexible enough to expand into new surfaces while maintaining a clear, ownable visual identity.

Brand Identity
Motion & Animation
Motion Design

Animation principles applied across the brand — bringing the identity to life through considered motion that reinforces hierarchy and guides attention.

Process
01
Brand Audit & Strategy
Evaluated existing brand equity, identified what to preserve and what to evolve. Defined the strategic direction before touching any visuals.
02
Visual Language Development
Built the visual system from the ground up — typography, color, spacing, iconography, and motion principles that give Neuron Digital a consistent, ownable identity.
03
Component System & Documentation
Translated the brand into a scalable UI component library with clear usage guidelines — ensuring the system could be handed off and extended by any team member.
Design System

A full component library and visual language — colour tokens, interactive states, and documented patterns built to scale across every Neuron surface.

Overview

Casablanca Records defined 1970s excess — home to KISS, Donna Summer, Parliament, and the Village People. A label built on spectacle and fearless self-expression.

The brief: give this brand a bright, electrifying personality that captures disco as a cultural force, not just a sound.

Texture & Atmosphere
Electrifying · Visceral · Alive
Applications
Concept
01
Disco as Futurism
In the 70s, disco was modernity — a vision of the future through sequins and strobes. The visual language needed that same charge: electric, luminous, unapologetically bold.
02
The Roster as a Compass
Every decision filtered through the acts: KISS, Donna Summer, Village People, Cher — a lineage stretching to Tiësto, SG Lewis, and KUNGS. Spectacle and soul in equal measure.
03
Neon as Identity
Warm, glowing, slightly imperfect — the kind of sign burning at 2am that pulls you in from across the street. Neon became the core visual metaphor for everything the label stood for.
Reflection

Working on a real cultural artifact forced me to think about what a brand feels like, not just what it looks like. The 70s constraint pushed me toward a bolder, more atmospheric visual language than I'd normally reach for.

✓ What worked

Leading with emotion and era rather than trying to modernise the brand. The neon treatment gave the identity an immediacy that felt true to the label's personality.

→ Next time

I'd push further into the full system — how does this identity live on merchandise, packaging, and digital? The sign is a strong anchor; I'd want to build more surfaces around it.
